The Payoff, Running

Lesson 2 made promises. Lessons 3–11 built the machine. This lesson presses run: the Bool/Binary swap executes, fraction arithmetic computes across the quotient, and the course's IOUs are settled.

The win for this lesson You can trace each Lesson 2 promise to the machinery that pays it, and you know what it means that the proofs normalize: transported programs produce values, quotient arithmetic evaluates, and nothing jams. This is the "so what" of the whole course, cashed.

Promise 1: the Bool/Binary swap — now it evaluates

The Lesson 2 scenario, replayed on the finished machine:

e : Bool ≃ Binary                    -- built by hand, once (to / from / inverse proofs)
p = ua e : Bool = Binary             -- a REAL path now: λ i → Glue …  (Lesson 11)

binaryMeet = transport Meet p boolMeet     -- the one-line structure port (Lesson 2)

binaryMeet.op one one                       -- press run:
  = transport unfolds structurally over the record   (pairs → componentwise, functions →
    inputs back / outputs forward, Glue → run e)     (Lesson 11's transport machine)
  = to (and (from one) (from one))
  = to (and true true)  =  to true  =  one           -- a VALUE. it ran.

In book-HoTT that call jammed on the opaque ua. Now ua e is a Glue-path, transport unfolds through the record type former by type former, hits the Glue rule, runs the dictionary, and delivers one. The proofs travelled too: binaryMeet.idem is a real, usable proof term for Binary. Prove once, transport anywhere, and execute — the full Lesson 2 sentence, with all three verbs true.

Promise 2: fractions — arithmetic across the quotient

The Lesson 8 quotient, doing its day job:

_+ℚ_ : ℚ → ℚ → ℚ
(a / b) +ℚ (c / d) = (a·d + c·b) / (b·d)      -- defined on representatives
… scale case: a proof that +ℚ respects 1/2 = 2/4 …   -- the one obligation

1/2 +ℚ 1/3                                     -- press run:
  = (1·3 + 1·2) / (2·3)  =  5/6                -- computes on the representatives

1/2 +ℚ 1/3  =  2/4 +ℚ 1/3                      -- and the QUOTIENT is honest:
  -- both sides evaluate; the scale-path makes them EQUAL, and transport
  -- along that path is usable in later proofs — no setoid bookkeeping anywhere.

This is what "quotients without setoid hell" means at runtime: you compute on representatives like a programmer, the declared paths guarantee the answers glue together like a mathematician, and the typechecker enforced (via the scale case) that no function ever depended on the representative. All three roles — computing, identifying, enforcing — active at once.

Promise 3: the structure identity principle, industrial-strength

The Meet record was a toy. The general form is the structure identity principle (SIP): for a whole class of structures (monoids, groups, orders, …), equivalence of underlying types + preservation of structure = identity of structures. The cubical Agda library ships this as a reusable theorem: hand it your structure, get transport-of-everything for free — proofs, programs, the lot, all computing. Representation independence is not a discipline anymore; it is a library function.

How far this scales, as a closing story: Brunerie proved (in HoTT) that a certain homotopy group of the 3-sphere is ℤ/nℤ for a specific n his proof constructed but did not name — and because cubical computes, "just evaluate n" became a meaningful plan. The number (it is 2) took years and heavy optimization to actually normalize, and later refinements got it computable in practice. The point of the story: in this system, even deep topology proofs are programs with return values. That is the same property your binaryMeet.op one one = one just used, at research scale.

Check yourself

Answer from memory first. The click gives immediate feedback.

Why does binaryMeet.op one one reach the value one in cubical but not in book-HoTT?

Both systems typecheck the term. In book-HoTT ua is a postulate and evaluation jams on it; in cubical ua e is a definition (a Glue-path) and transport's Glue rule runs the equivalence, so the term normalizes to one.

At runtime, what does "quotients without setoid hell" amount to for ℚ?

Arithmetic is defined and evaluated on representative pairs; the scale paths make equal fractions genuinely equal, and the per-function scale obligation already guaranteed no answer depends on the representative. No relation-passing, no congruence lemmas at use sites.

Say it in your own words

The course in three sentences: what did HoTT promise, what did book-HoTT lack, and what did cubical add?

HoTT reads equality as structured paths, and univalence makes identity of types coincide with equivalence — so proofs and programs transport across representations for free, and HITs give honest quotients. Book-HoTT delivers all of that as postulates, so the transported terms typecheck but jam: canonicity fails and nothing runs. Cubical redefines paths as functions out of an interval and adds transport, hcomp, and Glue, making funext and univalence computing theorems — so the same payoffs normalize to values, from binaryMeet.op one one = one to fraction arithmetic across the quotient.
